2. Fraction notation

Fraction notation CBSE-C6-AR-017

Fraction = a/b (b ≠ 0)

Numerator (a) — how many equal parts are taken. Denominator (b) — how many equal parts the whole is divided into. The denominator cannot be zero. Example: 5 shaded squares out of 12 is 5/12.

3. Chapter — Fractions

Equivalent fractions CBSE-C6-AR-018

a/b = (a × k)/(b × k) for any natural number k

Multiply numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number to get an equivalent fraction. Dividing numerator and denominator by their HCF gives the simplest form. Example: 8/12 simplifies to 2/3.

Addition of like fractions CBSE-C6-AR-019

a/c + b/c = (a + b)/c

When denominators match, add the numerators and keep the denominator. Example: 2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7.

Subtraction of like fractions CBSE-C6-AR-020

a/c − b/c = (a − b)/c

Same denominator: subtract numerators and keep the denominator. Example: 5/8 − 2/8 = 3/8.

Improper fraction to mixed number CBSE-C6-AR-021

Divide numerator by denominator; quotient = whole part, remainder/denominator = fractional part

Example: 14 ÷ 3 gives quotient 4 and remainder 2, so 14/3 = 4 2/3.

Example A — Equivalent fractions

Simplify 8/12.

HCF(8, 12) = 4. Divide numerator and denominator by 4.

2/3

Example B — Addition of like fractions

Find 2/7 + 3/7.

Same denominator 7: add numerators 2 + 3.

5/7

Example C — Subtraction of like fractions

Find 5/8 − 2/8.

Same denominator 8: subtract numerators 5 − 2.

3/8

Example D — Improper to mixed

Convert 14/3 to a mixed number.

4 2/3

5. Common mistakes

Do not add denominators when adding like fractions — only the numerators change.

Equivalent fractions need the same multiplier on top and bottom, or divide by the HCF to simplify.

For mixed numbers, the remainder becomes the new numerator; the denominator stays the same.

Unlike fractions (different denominators) need equivalent fractions first — not a Try It preset here.

Do not use this page for decimal ×÷, ratio, proportion, or percentage. Those are outside this milestone.
