Probability

Class 7

Introductory classical probability for CBSE Class 7 — equally likely outcomes, single events, and complements. Worked examples below use the same offline Probability engine as the AI Math Solver (Phase 8F).

1. Probability basics

Experiment — an action with uncertain results (toss a coin, roll a die).

Outcome — one possible result (head, tail, 4, even number, …).

Sample space — the set of all possible outcomes.

Event — a collection of outcomes we are interested in (e.g. “getting a head”, “rolling an even number”).

Favorable outcomes — outcomes that make the event happen.

Equally likely — each outcome has the same chance (fair coin, fair die, equal sections on a spinner).

2. Classical probability formula

Classical probability CBSE-C7-PR-001

P(E) = (number of favorable outcomes) ÷ (total number of equally likely outcomes)

Complementary probability CBSE-C7-PR-002

P(not E) = 1 − P(E)

“Not E” means the event does not happen. All probabilities here assume outcomes are equally likely.

3. Supported Class 7 question types

  • Fair coin — head or tail (P = 1/2 each).
  • Fair six-sided die — a specific face (P = 1/6).
  • Even or odd on a die — faces 2, 4, 6 or 1, 3, 5 (P = 3/6 = 1/2).
  • Complement — e.g. not rolling a 6, not getting a head.
  • Bag / spinner — when counts of colours or sections are given.
  • Explicit counts — “3 favorable outcomes out of 8 equally likely outcomes”.

Advanced topics such as two dice, conditional probability, card decks, and permutations/combinations are not covered on this Class 7 page.

4. Worked examples

Example A — Fair coin

What is the probability of getting a head when a fair coin is tossed?

Sample space: {H, T} → total = 2. Favorable for head = 1.

P(head) = 1/2

Example B — Die face

Find the probability of rolling a 4 on a fair die.

Sample space: {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} → total = 6. Favorable = 1.

P(4) = 1/6

Example C — Even number on a die

What is the probability of rolling an even number on a fair die?

Even faces: 2, 4, 6 → favorable = 3, total = 6.

P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2

Example D — Odd number on a die

Find the probability of getting an odd number when a fair die is rolled.

Odd faces: 1, 3, 5 → favorable = 3, total = 6.

P(odd) = 3/6 = 1/2

Example E — Complement

What is the probability of not rolling a 6 on a fair die?

P(6) = 1/6, so P(not 6) = 1 − 1/6 = 5/6.

Example F — Bag of balls

A bag has 3 red and 5 blue balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find P(red).

Total = 8, favorable for red = 3.

P(red) = 3/8
