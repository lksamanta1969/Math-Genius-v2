2. Addition of integers

Addition of Integers CBSE-C7-AR-001

a + (−b) = a − b

Adding the additive inverse of b is the same as subtracting b. So subtraction of integers is not a new operation — it is addition of the opposite. Example from the Formula Library: 7 + (−3) = 7 − 3 = 4.

Example A — Add a negative integer

Find 7 + (−3).

7 + (−3) = 7 − 3 = 4.

4

Example B — Subtract using the same identity

Find 5 − 8.

5 − 8 = 5 + (−8) = −3.

−3

3. Product of integers (sign rule)

Product of Integers (Sign Rule) CBSE-C7-AR-002

(−a)×(−b)=ab; (−a)×b=−(ab)

Same signs give a positive product. Different signs give a negative product. Formula Library examples: (−4)×(−5)=20 and (−4)×5=−20.

Example C — Same signs

Find (−4)×(−5).

Negative × negative is positive: 4 × 5 = 20.

20

Example D — Different signs

Find (−4)×5.

Negative × positive is negative: −(4 × 5) = −20.

−20

5. Common mistakes

a + (−b) is a − b, not a + b. The opposite of b is what you add.

5 − 8 is 5 + (−8), which is negative, not 8 − 5.

Negative × negative is positive. Do not keep a minus sign on the product of two negatives.

Negative × positive is negative. The size is a × b; the sign is minus.

Do not use this page for fraction ×÷, decimal ×÷, ratio, proportion, percentage, simple interest, or p/q rational form.
