1. How Class 6 Mensuration is organised

Shapes on this page — square, rectangle, and triangle only.

2. Perimeter and area — the idea

Perimeter — the total length of the boundary. Imagine walking all the way around a field: that path length is the perimeter. Units are units of length (cm, m, …).

Area — the measure of the enclosed region (how much surface is inside). Units are square units (cm², m², …).

They are different — perimeter and area are independent measures. A larger perimeter does not always mean a larger area.

Keep length and breadth (or base and height) in the same unit before you multiply or add.

3. Chapter — Perimeter and Area

Rectangle — opposite sides equal. Length is usually the longer side; breadth (width) is the shorter side.

Perimeter of a rectangle CBSE-C6-ME-001

P = 2(l + b)

P is perimeter, l is length, b is breadth. A rectangle has two lengths and two breadths, so P = l + b + l + b = 2(l + b). Perimeter units are units of length.

Area of a rectangle CBSE-C6-ME-004

A = l × b

A is area. Area measures the enclosed region. For a rectangle, area is length times breadth. Area units are square units.

Example A — Perimeter of a rectangle

If l = 12 cm and b = 5 cm, find the perimeter.

P = 2(12 + 5) = 2 × 17 = 34 cm.

P = 34 cm

Example B — Area of a rectangle

If l = 10 cm and b = 4 cm, find the area.

A = 10 × 4 = 40 cm².

A = 40 cm²

Square — all four sides are equal. Call one side a.

Perimeter of a square CBSE-C6-ME-002

P = 4a

All four sides of a square are equal, so perimeter is four times one side.

Area of a square CBSE-C6-ME-005

A = a²

Area of a square is the side multiplied by itself (a × a). A larger perimeter does not always mean a larger area.

Example C — Perimeter of a square

If a = 6 cm, find the perimeter.

P = 4 × 6 = 24 cm.

P = 24 cm

Example D — Area of a square

If a = 9 cm, find the area.

A = 9 × 9 = 81 cm².

A = 81 cm²

Triangle — a closed shape with three sides. Perimeter uses the three side lengths. Area uses a base and the matching perpendicular height.

Perimeter of a triangle CBSE-C6-ME-003

P = a + b + c

The perimeter of a triangle is the sum of its three sides a, b, and c.

Area of a triangle CBSE-C6-ME-006

A = ½ × base × height

Area of a triangle is half the product of its base and the corresponding perpendicular height. Height must be perpendicular to the chosen base — not a slanted side.

Example E — Perimeter of a triangle

Sides 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm. Find the perimeter.

P = 3 + 4 + 5 = 12 cm.

P = 12 cm

Example F — Area of a triangle

If base = 10 cm and height = 6 cm, find the area.

A = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm².

A = 30 cm²

5. Common mistakes

Do not mix units (for example cm with m) before you add or multiply.

Perimeter is a length (cm). Area is a square measure (cm²). Do not write cm for area.

For a triangle’s area, height must stand at right angles to the base you chose.

A square is a special rectangle, but use P = 4a and A = a² when all sides are equal — do not invent a different “circle” formula for a square.

Perimeter and area answer different questions. A bigger fence (perimeter) need not mean a bigger field (area).
