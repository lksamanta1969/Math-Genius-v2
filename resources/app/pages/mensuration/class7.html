2. Area of a parallelogram

Area of a parallelogram CBSE-C7-ME-001

A = b × h

b is the base. h is the corresponding perpendicular height (not a slanted side). Area units are square units.

Example A — Area of a parallelogram (lesson only)

If b = 8 cm and h = 5 cm, find the area.

A = 8 × 5 = 40 cm².

A = 40 cm²

This example is not a Try It preset. The Mensuration engine does not support parallelogram questions in this phase.

3. Circumference and area of a circle

Circumference of a circle CBSE-C7-ME-002

C = 2πr

Circumference is the perimeter of a circle. r is the radius. π may be taken as 22/7 or 3.14. Example: r = 7 cm, π = 22/7 ⇒ C = 2 × 22/7 × 7 = 44 cm.

Area of a circle CBSE-C7-ME-003

A = πr²

Area is π times the square of the radius. Example: r = 7 cm, π = 22/7 ⇒ A = 154 cm².

Example B — Circumference

Find the circumference of a circle with radius 7 cm, taking π = 22/7.

C = 2 × 22/7 × 7 = 44 cm.

C = 44 cm

Example C — Area of a circle

Find the area of a circle with radius 7 cm, taking π = 22/7.

A = 22/7 × 7² = 22/7 × 49 = 154 cm².

A = 154 cm²

5. Common mistakes

For a parallelogram, height must be perpendicular to the base you chose — not a slanted side.

Circumference is a length (cm). Circle area is a square measure (cm²).

Use the same units for radius before you substitute into 2πr or πr².

Do not use this page for volume, surface area, or 3D solids.

Do not expect Try It to solve parallelogram area — that formula is taught here, but the engine does not support it yet.
